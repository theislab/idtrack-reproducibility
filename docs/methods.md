# Methods of the benchmarks

This document states how idtrack is benchmarked: the principles every benchmark follows, what is asked of idtrack and by whom (the needs), which instrument measures each need, the benchmark families with their strata, truth, sizes and rules, the intervals, the known-limitations table, the weakness hunts, the comparison with other tools, the methodological choices still open and what could not be determined. Each family has its own methods document under [families/](families/) with its sets, results and how to reproduce it; the accuracy harness, which every family extends, is described in [the accuracy harness](harness.md); the rules idtrack follows and the rules of the judge are named in [rules](rules.md); how a family is added is in [adding](adding.md).

Locations are those of the data root of the configuration: `<truth>/<organism>/` (the one truth store), `<harness>/` (the harness's query sets and runs), `<families>/<family>/` (each family's sets and runs). The families' code is `idtrack_reproducibility.families.<family>`.

## Principles

These bind every family.

1. **Truth never comes from idtrack.** It comes from Ensembl's own files (the GTFs of every release, the core tables of releases 115 and 116 from the FTP MySQL dumps, the FTP `tsv/` cross-reference tables of releases 87 on, the assembly chain files), the archive BioMarts that still answer (tables fetched by the harness: pig 75, 77, 80, 105, 106, 108, 110, 112, 114, 115, 116; human 75, 80, 105, 110, 115, 116; mouse and rhesus 75, 80, 105, 110, 115; cynomolgus 105, 110, 115; the archives of 67, 85, 90, 98, 100, 102, 104 do not answer), Ensembl REST (`archive/id`, release 116; a check, never the main truth), and the authorities themselves: HGNC (complete set, withdrawn symbols and the archived quarterly sets), MGI (`MRK_List1.rpt` with withdrawn symbols, `MRK_List2.rpt`, `MGI_EntrezGene.rpt`), VGNC (gene sets of pig, rhesus, cynomolgus), NCBI (`gene_info`, `gene_history`, `gene2ensembl`, `gene2refseq`), RefSeq (release catalog), UniProt (`idmapping_selected`, `sec_ac.txt`, `delac_sp.txt`, isoform lists), miRBase, RFAM, RNAcentral, CCDS. Every fetched file is listed with URL, size, md5 and time in `<truth>/<organism>/SOURCES.tsv`: one truth store for the harness and every family; nothing is copied twice.
2. **Two kinds of truth are kept apart.** *Ensembl's own truth* (what Ensembl itself says at a release: GTF, core tables, BioMart, FTP `tsv/`) judges whether idtrack uses Ensembl correctly, and every rate a tree is accepted on is on it. *The authority's truth* (HGNC, MGI, VGNC, NCBI, UniProt, miRBase on their own) judges what a user who trusts the authority gets; where the two disagree, the disagreement is Ensembl's: it is measured beside idtrack's rate (the share of queries where the authority and Ensembl differ) and becomes a known limitation, never a wrong answer of idtrack.
3. **Exhaustive wherever the cost allows.** A query costs about 0.16 ms on `d4c2dda` (its travel-matrix runs: pig 192,250 queries in 30.5 s of query time, human 201,448 in 33.8 s; loading the human graph takes about 15 s per shard; `run.json` of `<harness>/answers/d4c2dda/<organism>/travel/`), so ten million queries are under an hour of one core; what limits a family is the truth computation, the storage of answers and the scoring, not idtrack. Each family says what it asks exhaustively, what it samples, the sample size and why ([Sizes and the cost model](#sizes-and-the-cost-model)).
4. **One call, both views.** From `71690ab` on, `API.convert` returns every candidate ranked with the best marked: one call is scored under `best` and under `all`. `d4c2dda` (`API.convert_identifier`) needs two calls; the adapter ([F0](#f0-the-common-machinery)) asks it twice.
5. **Outcomes**: `right`, `wrong`, `undetermined` (the truth leaves it open, with the reason), `timeout` (over the budget), `error` (an exception where an answer was expected), `expected_error` (the input is invalid and the documented error class was raised; families E and B6/B7), and, for other tools only, `not_supported` (family G). Rate = right / (right + wrong + error), as in the harness (with `expected_error` counted as right where a family has it); `undetermined` and `timeout` are reported apart and never counted.
6. **Intervals that do not overstate.** Wilson 95 % per query and, where one id or one lineage gives many queries, a cluster bootstrap over the unit that generated them ([Intervals](#intervals)); every wrong count is also given as the number of distinct units behind it.
7. **Every family measures the reference tree and every later tree** ([Measurement design](#measurement-design): exhaustively on the reference and the final tree, by its panel in between). The reference tree is `d4c2dda`; each tree is run on the graphs it built (the configured trees and graphs), with `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, and a later tree is compared with the reference answer by answer (`idtrack_reproducibility.families.compare`; the harness's `compare_scores`): no right -> wrong, no right -> error, every right -> timeout or -> undetermined explained, per stratum the rate equal or higher (on the clustered interval), every moved answer labelled with the judge of its line and the rule that causes it (rule [`truth_judges`](rules.md#truth_judges)). The hardest lineages ([A*](#a-the-hardest-lineages)) are read first; a family's report names the trees side by side.
8. **Room for what the hunts find.** Every accepted finding of a weakness hunt ([Weakness hunts](#weakness-hunts)) becomes (i) its reproducer as query lines of a published set `hunt_<n>` of the family it belongs to, with an expectation from the truth sources above (never the hunt's own belief), and (ii) when it is a pattern, a stratum of its family's generator, so that the whole class is measured, not the one example.

## Measurement design

A family is run **exhaustively** (every line of every published set) on two trees only: the reference tree `d4c2dda`, whose answers every change is compared with, and the **final tree** of a development, whose results are the ones published. Between them, each development stage is measured by the family's **panel**: a published set, built by the common machinery's `panel` command from the family's own sets, that holds

- every line that was ever `wrong` or `undetermined` on any tree measured so far (where a stage can most plausibly move an answer, in either direction);
- every reproducer of the weakness hunts that belongs to the family ([Weakness hunts](#weakness-hunts));
- a seeded sample, stratified by stratum and organism, of about 50,000 of the other lines per stratum and organism.

Why: an exhaustive pass of the families is hundreds of millions of lines per tree (A alone about 400 million rows of answers on `d4c2dda`), hours of query time and days of a shared queue, while a stage changes a small part of the answers. The panel keeps every line a stage has reason to move and bounds what it can break unseen: if a stage made a share p of the right lines of a stratum wrong, the sample of 50,000 would show none of them with probability (1 - p)^50,000, under 5 % for p of 6 in 100,000 or more. The final tree is then measured exhaustively, so that no published rate rests on a panel; the results index says of every row whether it is a panel result or an exhaustive one (`scope`). A smaller check for any tree, in a few hours on one job per organism, is the [modest set](modest.md): the harness's published sets, every hunt reproducer and 500 sampled lines per stratum of each family.

### How the frequencies will be measured

- **The web application**: once deployed, its api logs per request the endpoint, organism, argument shape (from/to release given or not, final database, explain, batch size) and the outcome status, never the identifiers; a monthly table of these counts re-weights the families' reports. Until then the families are unweighted and the web application's needs are covered by its endpoint list.
- **Users**: the GitHub issues and the tutorials are the only evidence; issue #85's example (`TP53`, `ENSG00000141510`, `NOT_A_REAL_GENE`, mixed in one dataset) is a stratum of [D4](#d-real-world-inputs).

## The families

Every family extends the accuracy harness: its query line, its truth readers (`idtrack_reproducibility.harness.make_queries.Truth`, `harness_lib`), its scoring rules (`set`, `set_exact`, `broad`, `locus`, `locus_mapped`, `apart`, `support`, `spellings`, `excluded`; [scoring rules](harness.md#the-query-line-and-the-scoring-rules)), and its convention that a published set is fixed and a change of truth is a revision set. Nothing replaces the harness; the families widen it. The parts every family has, and where they live, are in [adding](adding.md#layout).

**Cost model** (seconds per query by kind on `d4c2dda`, from `outcomes.tsv.gz` of `<harness>/answers/d4c2dda/<organism>/kinds/`, pig and human): Ensembl gene ids 0.10 to 0.53 ms mean (split ids 0.5 to 0.8 ms), history kinds 0.14 to 0.39 ms, names 0.12 to 0.43 ms (names of several genes 0.8 to 3.8 ms), external ids 0.11 to 0.16 ms, **transcript and translation ids 5.9 to 18 ms**, unknown versions 3.6 to 13 ms, strings that are nothing 13 to 32 ms; the slowest single query in any kind 0.83 s; loading takes 15 s per shard (human graph); `d4c2dda` needs two calls per line (`best`, `all`), `71690ab` one. ### F0: the common machinery

Built first; every family uses it ([F0's document](families/f0.md)). Code: `idtrack_reproducibility.families` (the modules `lines`, `adapter`, `runner`, `scorer`, `report`, `compare`, `explain`, `causes`, `limitations`); data under `<families>/<family>/` (`queries/<organism>/<set>`, `runs/<tree>/<organism>/`, `logs/`); truth in the one store `<truth>/<organism>/`.

- **Query lines**: the harness's line (`qid`, `kind`, `id`, `from_release`, `to_release`, `final_database`, `strategy`, `rule`, `cmp`, `expect`, `broad`, `locus`, `note`, and the corrected judge's fields) plus `family`, `stratum`, `unit` (the cluster of the bootstrap: gene id, lineage component, name, dataset) and, for families E and B6/B7, `expect_error` (the error class the docs promise). Sets of more than a million lines are Parquet (`<set>.parquet` with the manifest's sha256), the others `jsonl.gz`. A published set is fixed; revisions are revision sets, for every family as for the harness ([the query line](families/f0.md#the-query-line)).
- **The adapter**: one function per api generation that asks a line and returns the answer the scorer reads (`targets`, `apart`, `status`, `match`, `tie`, `from_release` used, `error` type, `seconds`, and with `explain` the path): `d4c2dda` through `API.convert_identifier` (two calls for `best` and `all`; read by the harness's own `score.targets_of`), `71690ab` and later through `API.convert` (one call, both views). Its check: the harness's published sets of all five organisms asked through it and scored by F0's scorer give the stored harness runs' outcomes, reasons and answers line by line ([the check](families/f0.md#the-check-against-the-harnesss-scorer)).
- **The runner**: shards by `crc32(qid) % n` (as the harness's `score`), resumes by skipping answered qids, loads the graph once per shard, `gc.freeze()`, `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, a per-call budget (`71690ab`: `budget_seconds`; `d4c2dda`: the harness's timeout), writes Parquet answers per shard.
- **The scorer**: the harness's rules, vectorised over Parquet (families of tens of millions of lines need columnar scoring), checked against the harness's scorer on the harness's sets (identical outcomes); plus the rules `expect_error` (the documented error class is right; another class or an answer is wrong), `safe` (for inputs whose right answer is open, e.g. B7: the gene or nothing is right, another gene is wrong) and `relation` (family E8: a relation between answers instead of a truth).
- **Reports**: per family, organism and stratum: n, right, wrong, undetermined, timeout, error, rate with Wilson and clustered intervals, distinct units behind the wrong answers, seconds p50/p95/p99/max; the wrong answers with their causes (the cause classifier `idtrack_reproducibility.families.causes`, extended per family; classes a to e, [causes](families/f0.md#causes)); the comparison of two scored runs with every moved answer (`families.compare --moved`).

### A*: the hardest lineages

Judged first and reported apart ([A*'s document](families/astar.md)). The exhaustive family A measures history travel on average; A* measures it where it is hardest and where a regression would hide in an average.

**Selection** (from Ensembl's history and GTFs only; `idtrack_reproducibility.families.astar.selection`): every gene id of the window gets a score per pattern, and the set is the union over patterns of every id at or above the pattern's threshold, plus the ids of the hunts' findings and the human ids behind the weakest cell of the travel matrix (backward within GRCh37). The patterns of the design:

| pattern | how it is found | threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| large lineages | connected components of the cross-id event graph (old != new) | every id of every component with 5 ids or more |
| many events | sessions with a non-self event of the id | every id with 3 or more |
| split then merged back | two event paths from one id that meet again in a later id | all |
| merged then split | the mirror image | all |
| retired and resurrected | the id leaves the GTF and comes back, or an event to nothing followed by one from nothing (rule [`resurrection`](rules.md#resurrection)) | all |
| gaps | absent from the GTF of a release between two where it is present, without an event | all |
| version jumps and decreases | a self event or consecutive GTFs whose version rises by more than one, or falls | all |
| moved | the same id on another sequence, or on the other strand, at another release (GTF) | all |
| across handoffs | lineages with an event in a handoff session, and ids that change place across it by the chain files | all |
| chains of renames | the longest id chains (one successor each) | every chain of length 3 or more |
| successors overlapping other genes | a successor whose place at its release overlaps another living gene on the same strand | all |
| events contradicting tables | an event whose version or release does not match the GTF (rule [`event_version`](rules.md#event_version)'s case), sessions mislabelled (pig 106 -> "104", human 114 -> "114") | all |
| non-reference copies | lineages with genes on patches, alternative loci or LRG records | all |
| long-lived with many versions | the ids with the most distinct versions | top 1 % |

The selection as built refined some patterns where the first measurement showed them reading noise and added patterns from the hunts (branches while the id lives on, hh-1; copies on haplotypes or patches whose history ends, R6 and J1; Y PAR genes, R4); its patterns, thresholds and sizes are in [A*'s document](families/astar.md).

**Queries**: each selected id from every release it exists at to every release of the window (the full matrix), forward, backward and in place; versioned and unversioned; with and without `from_release`; `best` and `all` (one call from `71690ab` on); with a name database as `final_database` (HGNC Symbol, MGI Symbol, UniProtKB Gene Name or VGNC, as the travel matrix chooses) at every target with a BioMart table; transcripts and translations of the selected genes from their releases to the gene at every target. Exhaustive over the selected ids.

**Truth**: the harness's strict walk with living ids, the broad walk with returned ids, copies apart from release 82 on, the `locus` rule with places mapped across assemblies (rules of the judge: [`id_lives_on`](rules.md#id_lives_on), [`resurrection`](rules.md#resurrection), [`judge_copies_apart`](rules.md#judge_copies_apart), [`judge_corrections`](rules.md#judge_corrections)). **Plus a second, independent reading for these ids only**: Ensembl REST `archive/id` (release 116) for every id of the set and its `possible_replacement` chain; a line where the walk and REST differ is `undetermined` with both and listed apart (the history reading itself is under test where it is hardest).

**Report** (apart, per organism, per pattern, per id): the hard-ids table (one row per id: pattern, lineage size, queries, right/wrong/undetermined, the wrong cells as origin -> target), the rate per pattern with the clustered interval, and the explained paths of the wrong answers (`explain=True`). A later tree is read on A* before anything else; any right -> wrong there is explained before the tree's other results are read.

### E: robustness

Robustness, adversarial inputs and metamorphic relations ([E's document](families/e.md)). The docs and docstrings of `71690ab` are the specification (`API.convert`'s "Raises", rule [`typed_api`](rules.md#typed_api)'s error hierarchy, rule [`missing_values`](rules.md#missing_values)); an input whose documented outcome is an error is right only with that error class (`expect_error`). Where a decided rule of idtrack's next version changes the expected answer, a line is scored under two judges: `now` (the documented behaviour of `71690ab`) and `decided` (the rule) ([rules](rules.md)).

- **E1 missing values**: every string of rule `missing_values` (`""`, `nan`, `NaN`, `NA`, `N/A`, `None`, `null`, in every case, with white space), single and in batches at every position, with and without `from_database` (named: looked up).
- **E2 malformed strings**: control characters, NUL, emoji, right-to-left marks, mixed scripts, very long strings (10^3, 10^5, 10^6 characters), many separators (the dash/underscore permutation search), numbers and scientific notation (`1e5`, `1.00E+05`, NCBI ids with leading zeros), ids with inner spaces, injection strings for the web (SQL, HTML, path traversal, format strings).
- **E3 types**: `None`, int, float NaN, bytes, list, dict, a str as a batch, NumPy and pandas inputs of every dtype (rule `typed_api`'s accepted types), single and batch.
- **E4 another organism**: every organism's current gene ids, transcript ids and a sample of its symbols asked in every other organism's graph (ids: nothing; a symbol that is also a name in this organism: this organism's gene, by the truth of B).
- **E5 arguments at and beyond their bounds**: releases below, at and above the window, non-integers, booleans, negative; `to_release` before `from_release`; databases unknown, in another case, with white space, the synonym prefix (rule [`no_synonym_target`](rules.md#no_synonym_target)); budgets 0, negative, NaN, infinite, huge; `infer_source` modes; harmonization arguments.
- **E6 budgets**: the 200 slowest queries of every family under budgets from 1 state to the default: either the documented budget error or the answer of the unbounded call, never a partial answer; a larger budget never changes a finished answer.
- **E7 determinism and concurrency**: the harness's published sets of every organism (and a 100,000-line sample of A and B) under hash seeds 0, 1, 2 and random; in 1, 2 and 8 processes over one memory-mapped graph (rule `process_workers`); in 8 threads sharing one `API`; in shuffled order; as batches of 1, 100, 10,000 against single calls; with duplicates; after a call that raised: canonical answers byte-identical (the regression corpus's canonical form).
- **E8 metamorphic relations** (no truth; each violation listed with its explained path): identity (R -> R gives the id itself, every A1 line); composition (R1 -> R2 -> R3 against R1 -> R3, judged leg by leg against the history as the travel matrix's checks do); return (an unchanged id goes back to itself); versioned and unversioned forms of a current id give one gene; a unique name and its case variant give one gene; white space trimmed equals untrimmed; `best` within `all`; `batch[i]` equals the single call; adding an unknown id to a batch changes no other item; `final_database=X` then X back to genes contains the gene; `infer_source` unchanged by order and duplicates; harmonization unchanged by feature and dataset order (union); **the path is valid**: every history step of an explained path is an event of `stable_id_event` between the releases it names, every bridge is an xref both genes held at its release, the last node is the candidate (this one against Ensembl's tables: a truth).
- **E9 the web layer**: a sample of 10,000 lines of every family asked through the web application's api (container, local graphs) gives the package's answer field for field (nothing rounded or reworded); every limit at and beyond its bound (`batch_max`, `identifier_max_length`, `body_max_bytes`, `response_max_bytes`, rate) answers its documented status.

Sizes: E1 to E5 are enumerations (a few thousand lines each per organism, every one asked); E6 to E8 as stated; all five organisms.

### F: harmonization

Harmonization of count matrices (`HarmonizeFeatures`).

- **F1 synthetic with known truth**: per organism, datasets built from the truth: for source releases spread over the window (the harness's releases R), features named as the GTF of R names them (Ensembl ids, versioned ids, symbols, a mix), drawn to include every history class (unchanged, renamed, merged, split, retired, resurrected), moved names, ambiguous names, made-unique names, features absent from the graph, duplicates; counts drawn per feature (seeded). The expected harmonized matrix (genes of T x cells) follows from A's and B's truth: a feature reaches its true gene(s); merges as `merge="sum"` and `"flag"` define; union and intersection over datasets. Judged: each feature's assignment (right/wrong/undetermined by A's and B's rules), count conservation (counts kept, moved, dropped, by class, against the expected), the matrix against the expected for the features whose truth is decided, `obs`, `obsm`, `uns` and `layers` kept (issue #85), invariance to feature and dataset order, the result cache keyed by the content digest (rule [`cache_by_digest`](rules.md#cache_by_digest)).
- **F2 the atlas project's matrices through `HarmonizeFeatures`**: the 33 matrices' feature lists with their counts harmonized to 115 and judged by D1's locus truth, weighted by counts.
- **F3 scale**: 10^3 to 10^6 cells, 2 x 10^4 to 6 x 10^4 features, 2 to 30 datasets: time and memory (family H).

### H: performance

Performance per family ([H's document](families/h.md)). Every family's answers carry `seconds` per query: latency p50/p95/p99/max per family, stratum and organism, read from the runs themselves (no separate run). Beside it, in their own jobs, on one node type, the reference tree and the tree measured interleaved, three repetitions: load time and resident memory per graph; batch scaling (`convert_many` of 1 to 100,000 identifiers: time per item, peak memory); memory over 10^6 mixed queries; processes over a shared memory-mapped graph (rule `process_workers`): throughput for 1 to 16 workers; the web container under load (an open-loop generator such as `oha` or `locust`, at fixed rates, convert and batch endpoints: p50/p99, errors, the limits holding); harmonization time and memory over F3's sizes; builds (time, peak memory, bytes received).

## Intervals

- **Wilson** 95 % per query, as the harness gives it (`ci_low`, `ci_high` of every `scores.tsv`; `wilson_low`, `wilson_high` of every `report.tsv`). It treats queries as independent.
- **Cluster bootstrap** where one id or one lineage gives many queries (A, A*, the travel strata of B, C at many releases): resampling the unit that generated the queries (the gene id, the lineage component, the name, the dataset: the line's `unit`), 2,000 resamples, seed 20261118 (`boot_low`, `boot_high` of every `report.tsv`). This is the interval a tree is accepted on.
- **Units behind the wrong answers**: every wrong count is also given as the number of distinct units behind it (`units_wrong` of every `report.tsv`; the travel matrix counts the ids behind its wrong answers).
- Rate = right / (right + wrong + error) (with `expected_error` counted as right); `undetermined` and `timeout` apart. A rate of 1.0 with n lines is read with its lower bound, not as a certainty.

## Known limitations

**Structure**: one entry per limitation, with an id (`L-<name>`), a title, *what* idtrack cannot answer or answers with a stated uncertainty, *its measured size per organism* (the count and share from a named family, strata, run and graph digest; never an estimate), *its cause* (Ensembl's data, an authority's data, a rule of idtrack named by [rules](rules.md), the scope of the design), *what a user can do* (an argument, a database, a check, another resource), *the family that measures it* (so every later tree re-measures it), *since and until* (the version where it was measured, and the rule or version that removes it, if one does). Groups: history (no successor and bridges; places across assemblies without chain files; copies on patches before release 82; resurrections; composition that does not hold across splits and merges; the graph's window and snapshot), names (several genes, `best` choosing the better-supported; family labels; spellings; mangled and Unicode forms; projected human symbols in mouse), external identifiers (databases not in the graph; final conversion at old releases bounded by Ensembl's xrefs then; isoforms; Ensembl against the authority), inputs (missing values refused; another organism's ids), source inference (releases with equal gene lists), harmonization, performance (budgets), organisms (organisms without versioned ids are not supported, rule [`versioned_organisms`](rules.md#versioned_organisms); organisms outside the five untested). The seed list came from the reading of every wrong answer of the harness (the causes of [F0's classifier](families/f0.md#causes)) and the harness's [limits](harness.md#limits), each re-measured by its family before it is written.

**Source and publication**: one table, `results/limitations/limitations.tsv`, generated from the families' causes and reports (`idtrack-repro limitations`, `idtrack_reproducibility.families.limitations` with each family's own map; no number typed by hand). Its columns: `limitation`, `group`, `title`, `what`, `organism`, `answers`, `units`, `of_answers` (the lines of those strata scored on the tree), `share`, `family`, `strata`, `run`, `commit`, `graph_digest`, `class`, `cause`, `rule`, `user_action`, `fixed_by`, `measured_by`, `since`. The same table is the source for idtrack's documentation page of limitations and for the web application's About page (shipped beside the graphs), so that both name the release of idtrack and the graphs their numbers come from.

## Weakness hunts

The families measure what was foreseen; the hunts look for what was not ([the weakness hunts](hunts.md)).

**Angles**: history (the hardest lineages), inputs (what users type and paste), regions (hard places of genomes) have run ([History](hunts.md#history), [Inputs](hunts.md#inputs), [Regions](hunts.md#regions)); then, once the families exist: external identifiers (old and unusual accessions, databases not in the graph, final conversion at old releases), names over time (symbols moved between genes, the authorities' histories against Ensembl's), the explained path (paths that do not match their answer or Ensembl's events), concurrency and the web layer (workers, limits, schemas, the path view against the package), harmonization (issue #85's class: what is lost between input and output objects), the docs against behaviour (every promise of a docstring tested), and slow queries (inputs that exhaust budgets).

**Reproducer format**: one row per finding in the angle's `findings.tsv` (id, severity, organisms, how many, the reproducer, the expected answer with its truth's source, the answer got on the tree named, the cause, what it became, which family and stratum measure it now), a runnable reproducer (`python -m idtrack_reproducibility.hunts.reproduce <angle> <id>`: loads the configured tree's graph, makes the call, prints expected and got; [reproducing](hunts.md#reproducing)), and the finding's query lines in the families' format (`hunt_<angle>_<n>`, with `expect` from a truth source, never from the hunter's belief).

**Triage** (per round): duplicates by cause are merged; then each finding becomes one of: a fix of idtrack under a rule it already follows, a new rule of idtrack ([rules](rules.md)), a known limitation (with its size measured by the family), a truth error (a revision set with evidence), or not a defect (with the reason). Every accepted finding's query lines are published as a set `hunt_<n>` of its family (fixed once published) and scored with it on the reference tree and every later tree; a pattern becomes a stratum of the family's generator (a new set), so its whole class is measured.

**Rounds and stopping**: a round is one full pass of every active angle on one tree, starting from the previous round's findings; a round's findings are measured before the tree is accepted; hunting on a tree stops when a round finds nothing new (no finding that is not a duplicate by cause) in any angle, and every angle's report says what its last pass covered.

## Open methodological choices

Choices that change what users get or what the comparison means; the families score both ways until they are made.

- **Q1 Unicode and spreadsheet forms** (B6, B7): should `TNFα`, `IL-1β`, `7-Sep`, `1-Mar` be read as the genes they stand for, or refused? For reading them: users paste such lists (the spreadsheet problem is documented: Zeeberg 2004, Ziemann 2016), a refusal loses the gene, and the forms are a closed set. Against: every normalisation is a guess that can name a wrong gene (`MARCH1` is no longer a symbol; `1-Mar` can be a date in a column of dates); "nothing" is never wrong. Invisible characters, byte-order marks and Unicode dashes are decided for idtrack's next version (rule [`unicode_normalised`](rules.md#unicode_normalised)); spreadsheet dates and Greek letters are not. The families score with the `safe` rule (the gene or nothing is right, another gene wrong) and report the recovery rate apart.
- **Q2 an accession version that never existed** (C2, C3): a RefSeq or UniProt version that the table never had: the accession's gene (as rule [`version_newer_than_graph`](rules.md#version_newer_than_graph) answers an Ensembl version newer than the graph; `d4c2dda` cuts the version of an accession, rule [`accession_version_cut`](rules.md#accession_version_cut)) or nothing (as rule [`unknown_version`](rules.md#unknown_version) answers an unknown Ensembl version)? For the gene: RefSeq versions advance between Ensembl releases, so a newer version is common in fresh tables. For nothing: an unknown version is evidence that the input is not what it says. Decided since: rule [`accession_versions`](rules.md#accession_versions), by which family C judges.
- **Q3 where the comparison lives** (G): as a family of this repository, or as a repository of its own that imports this package's truth readers and scorer. For this repository: one home for the truth, the scorer and every result, nothing copied (a copy of the truth or the scorer elsewhere is what the one-home rule forbids). For a repository of its own: G carries an R/Bioconductor environment and large caches of web responses that change on the services' rhythm, not idtrack's. Either way the truth readers and the scorer have one home, here.
- **Q4 graphs of release 116** for the comparison (the current services answer at 116): building them (five builds, about 30 minutes to a few hours each over HTTPS) is the fair counterpart; without them the comparison uses only the lines whose truth is the same at 115 and 116.
